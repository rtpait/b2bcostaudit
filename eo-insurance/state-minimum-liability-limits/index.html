State-by-State E&O Minimum Liability Limits & Statutory vs. Contractual Coverage Matrix

⚖️ Statutory Law vs. Commercial MSA Reality: Which States Actually Require E&O Insurance? 50-State + DC Single Authority Table
  • 🏛️ STATUTORY LICENSE MANDATES
    No US state mandates general E&O insurance for all B2B corporations. Statutory state law requires E&O only for specific state-licensed professions:
    • 12 Mandatory Real Estate E&O States: CO, ID, IA, KY, LA, MS, NE, NM, ND, RI, SD, TN ($100k/$300k to $300k/$500k state group policies).
    • Resident Insurance Producers: Oregon (ORS Ch. 744) mandates $300,000 / $600,000 E&O.
    • Licensed Attorneys: Oregon ($300k State Bar PLF) and Idaho ($100k/$300k Bar Rule 229).
    • Licensed Healthcare Providers: CO, CT, KS, PA, WI statutory medical malpractice minimums.
  • 🛡️ STATE CYBER SAFE HARBORS
    For B2B SaaS, IT, and Consulting firms, state risk is governed by Statutory Cybersecurity Tort Safe-Harbor Laws (Ohio ORC § 1354.01, Utah Code § 78B-4-702, Connecticut Gen. Stat. § 36a-701b, Tennessee TIPA—which shield businesses conforming to NIST/CIS/ISO 27001 from punitive tort damages) and strict statutory penalty regimes (California CCPA/CPRA § 1798.150, NYDFS 23 NYCRR 500, Illinois BIPA).
  • 📝 B2B CONTRACTUAL FLOORS
    Even where state statute is silent, State CIO IT Procurement Contracts require $1M/$2M to $2M/$4M Tech E&O + $2M–$5M Cyber, and private Enterprise MSAs demand $2M/$2M (SOC 2) to $5M/$5M (HIPAA/Banking) across all 50 states.
